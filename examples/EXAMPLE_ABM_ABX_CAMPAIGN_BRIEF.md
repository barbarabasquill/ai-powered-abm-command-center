## Campaign brief example

*EXECUTIVE CAMPAIGN BRIEF  |  IDENTITY SECURITY SOLUTIONS*

### ABM / ABX Campaign Brief

**Target Account Cohort(s):** Enterprise Financial Services & Insurance (Cohort A) and Cloud-Heavy Technology & SaaS (Cohort C) \| 5,000+ Employees \| Privileged Access Management

- Company / solution: Acme Identity Cloud (fictional company) — Privileged Access Management (PAM)
- Industry: Financial Services & Insurance; Cloud-Heavy Technology & SaaS
- Company size: Enterprise (5,000+ employees)
- Solution focus: Privileged access management for human, administrative and cloud/DevOps access

**Account-Selection Signals (Proposed criteria — not verified account-level signals):** 

- Technographic: Multiple or legacy vault/PAM tools, heavy multi-cloud or SaaS administration, large IT/DevOps admin populations, or fragmented identity tooling.
- Intent: Third-party (Bombora) and 6sense topic research tied to privileged access, identity security, least privilege, and just-in-time access. Topic list and thresholds are Pending Validation.
- Business triggers: New CISO or IAM leadership, audit or regulatory findings, cloud migration, M&A integration, identity-related incidents in the peer set, or IAM/PAM hiring.
- Qualification: ICP fit + account value + signal recency + signal confidence + reachable buying group; confirm manually before any personalized claim.

**Campaign type:** Account-based new-pipeline creation and opportunity development

**Planning horizon:** 90-day activation + 90-day pipeline-tracking tail (Proposed)

**Campaign Start Date:** To be confirmed

**Campaign End Date:** To be confirmed (activation end = start + 90 days; tracking tail ends + 90 days)

**Campaign Name:** Proposed working name — Privileged Access, Under Control

**Salesforce CID:** Pending — not yet assigned


### Campaign Overview

Engage a focused set of enterprise financial services, insurance and cloud-heavy SaaS organizations that manage large populations of privileged human, service and cloud identities. Align security, identity, infrastructure and risk stakeholders around a consultative privileged access conversation, supported by validated account signals and coordinated Sales and Marketing outreach.

| ELEMENT | PROPOSED CAMPAIGN DIRECTION |
| --- | --- |
| Primary objective | Generate new qualified pipeline for Acme Identity Cloud PAM among named Tier 1 and Tier 2 enterprise accounts. |
| Pilot success metric | Illustrative, Pending Validation: 10–18 Sales-accepted qualified meetings in the 90-day activation window, with the primary measure being sales-accepted opportunities created. No baseline, ACV or win-rate data exists yet; confirm all targets with Sales and RevOps. |
| Desired progression | Engaged buying group → qualified meeting → discovery → tailored offer session or demonstration → sales-accepted opportunity → pipeline progression. |
| Operating cadence | Weekly account and pipeline review with Marketing, BDR/SDR and Account Executives; checkpoints at days 30, 60 and 90, and a pipeline review at day 180. |


#### The Opportunity

Test whether enterprise security and identity leaders are actively prioritizing tighter control of privileged access (standing privileges, cloud and DevOps admin access, credential sprawl and audit evidence) and whether Acme Identity Cloud can earn consultative conversations in both cohorts. The pilot also builds the repeatable signal-to-meeting-to-opportunity motion for future cohorts.


#### Executive Decisions Required

| DECISION | PROPOSED DIRECTION |
| --- | --- |
| Audience | Approve the two target cohorts, the Tier 1 / Tier 2 account counts (Illustrative: 10–15 Tier 1, 40–60 Tier 2) and named Sales ownership. |
| Offer | Approve three offer pathways, matched to cohort and persona rather than one generic demo. |
| Investment | Approve the total campaign budget (currently not approved) and channel caps; the allocation in this brief is proportional only. |
| Operating model | Confirm Marketing, BDR/SDR, AE and Sales leadership responsibilities, alert SLAs and executive sponsors. |
| Measurement | Approve sourced vs. influenced attribution rules, qualified-meeting definition and the reporting owner. |

Evidence standard: ROI, cost-savings, risk-reduction, compliance and customer-outcome claims require approved product, finance or customer proof. No such claims are made in this brief. Product capabilities referenced are to be confirmed against approved documentation.


### Account Strategy & Buying Group Focus


#### Ideal Customer Profiles (ICPs)

| DIMENSION | TARGETING GUIDANCE |
| --- | --- |
| Firmographics | Financial services, insurance, and cloud-heavy technology / SaaS companies with 5,000+ employees; prioritize organizations with complex, regulated or multi-cloud environments. Size thresholds are assumptions pending validation. |
| Technographics | Multiple PAM, vault or IAM tools; multi-cloud or large SaaS footprints; extensive DevOps and infrastructure admin teams; signs of credential sprawl or manual access workflows. Source: 6sense / enrichment data, to be validated. |
| Priority triggers | Relevant third-party or 6sense intent, security or IAM leadership change, IAM/PAM hiring, audit or regulatory preparation, cloud migration or M&A activity. |
| Qualification rule | Score fit + account value + signal recency + signal confidence + accessible buying group; confirm manually before personalized claims. |


#### Target Account Cohorts

| COHORT | PROFILE | HYPOTHESIS TO TEST (PROPOSED) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cohort A: Financial Services & Insurance | Banks, insurers, asset managers and payments firms with 5,000+ employees and regulated environments. | Audit evidence, access governance and privileged-account control are recurring priorities; lead with risk review and audit-readiness conversations. |
| Cohort C: Cloud-Heavy Technology & SaaS | Software, platform and digital businesses with 5,000+ employees and large cloud and DevOps footprints. | Cloud admin access, secrets and engineering-speed access are the pain points; lead with a technical cloud access workflow demonstration. |
| Future: Healthcare | Retained as a possible expansion cohort. | Not in scope for this pilot; evaluate after the day-90 review. |


#### Account Tiering and ABM Motion

| TIER | ILLUSTRATIVE SIZE | MOTION AND TREATMENT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 | 10–15 accounts | 1:1. Account brief, buyer map, executive sponsor, personalized outreach, and Reachdesk executive engagement for accounts with validated signals. |
| Tier 2 | 40–60 accounts | 1:few by cohort and trigger. Cohort-specific LinkedIn and email, persona-based BDR cadence, and trigger-aware content. |
| Tier 3 / ICP-fit | Remaining in-market ICP accounts | 1:many. Awareness, retargeting and nurture; promote to Tier 2 or Tier 1 when signals and buying-group engagement warrant. |


#### Buying Committee and Message Priorities

| ROLE | LIKELY PRIORITY / MESSAGE ANGLE |
| --- | --- |
| Economic buyer (CISO / CIO) | Reduce identity-driven risk, demonstrate control coverage and build a defensible business case. |
| Decision maker (VP/Director of IAM or Security Architecture) | Program efficiency, platform fit, operating model and time to value. |
| Technical evaluator (IAM/PAM engineers, security and cloud architects) | Architecture, integration, coverage of cloud and hybrid assets, and workflow impact. |
| Champion (PAM / IAM program owner) | Credible internal story, fewer manual steps and fast, visible wins. |
| Influencers (GRC / audit, IT operations, DevOps / SRE) | Audit evidence, operational burden and developer experience. |
| Procurement / vendor risk | Vendor due diligence, security review and commercial process; engage early in Tier 1 opportunities. |
| Potential blockers (incumbent tool owners, application owners) | Switching effort, rollout disruption and change fatigue; address with a phased approach and approved proof. |

Segmentation: Use 1:1 for Tier 1 accounts with validated signals and engaged buying-group members, 1:few by cohort for Tier 2, and 1:many for awareness and retargeting. Buyer maps are built from verified contacts only; unknown roles stay flagged as gaps for BDR research.


### Offer(s) & Conversion Pathways

Offer architecture: Three distinct, buyer-relevant offers rather than one generic demo. Route each account to the offer that fits its cohort, role and signals. Demonstrations are used where a technical evaluation is the right next step.

| OFFER | PROPOSED EXPERIENCE / PRIMARY CTA |
| --- | --- |
| Offer #1: Privileged Access Risk Review (all cohorts) | Consultative working session to map privileged access, standing privileges and control gaps. CTA: Book a privileged access risk review. |
| Demo #2: Cloud & DevOps Access Workflow (Cohort C) | Guided demonstration of how privileged access is requested, granted and audited across cloud and engineering workflows, using only approved, live product capabilities. CTA: Request a guided workflow demo. |
| Offer #3: Audit-Readiness Working Session (Cohort A) | Practical session on privileged-access evidence, session oversight and access review workflows, tailored to regulatory context. CTA: Schedule an audit-readiness session. |

| STAGE | CONVERSION PATHWAY (PROPOSED) |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Signal | Validated 6sense / Bombora intent, trigger or buying-stage movement; ICP-fit confirmed. |
| 2. Engage | Marketing (ads, content, landing page) and BDR/AE outreach to the relevant personas. |
| 3. Convert | Offer selected by cohort and role; meeting booked via approved scheduling link. |
| 4. Advance | Qualified meeting → discovery → tailored session or demo → sales-accepted opportunity, with CRM updates at each step. |

Proof points: Approved product demonstrations, architecture diagrams, relevant customer stories, analyst or third-party validation and verified comparisons. All proof must be approved and catalogued in Seismic before use. No customer results or ROI figures are asserted in this brief.


### Coordinated Activation


#### Sales & Marketing Plays

| OWNER / MOTION | ACTION & HANDOFF |
| --- | --- |
| Marketing │ 1:1 | Create account briefs and buyer maps for Tier 1; personalize landing pages, ads and executive content; coordinate Reachdesk gifting and direct mail with Sales. |
| Marketing │ 1:few | Build cohort-specific LinkedIn campaigns, landing pages and content; run trigger-aware messaging and retargeting through 6sense; nurture engaged contacts in Marketo. |
| Marketing │ 1:many | Run awareness, SEO, review-site and retargeting programs; promote accounts to higher tiers when signals strengthen. |
| BDR / SDR │ 1:few | Run persona-based multi-touch cadences in Salesloft or Outreach, grounded in verified signals; offer a relevant working session, not a generic pitch. |
| AE │ 1:1 | Own Tier 1 outreach: executive notes or short personalized video, sponsor engagement, and tailored follow-up connected to an approved proof point. |
| Reachdesk │ executive and event engagement | Use gifting and direct mail selectively for Tier 1 executives, post-event follow-up, meeting activation and opportunity acceleration, within approved gifting policy. |
| Marketing + Sales │ orchestration | Review engagement and buying-stage movement weekly; route high-intent accounts to owners with next-best actions; log outcomes in Salesforce. |


#### Touch Sequence

| TOUCH | PURPOSE |
| --- | --- |
| Touch 1 │ Relevance | Introduce the observed privileged access challenge, framed by cohort and trigger, with a practical insight or checklist. |
| Touch 2 │ Credibility | Share an approved customer example, technical resource or risk-review discussion relevant to the persona. |
| Touch 3 │ Conversion | Invite the stakeholder to the matching offer: risk review, workflow demo or audit-readiness session. |
| Touch 4 │ Acceleration (Tier 1) | Executive follow-up, Reachdesk gesture or sponsor outreach to broaden the buying group and secure a next step. |


#### 90-Day Activation Phases (Proposed)

| PHASE | FOCUS |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 │ Prepare | Finalize account lists, signals, buyer maps, assets, routing and tracking; brief Sales. |
| Days 15–45 │ Engage | Launch 1:few and 1:1 plays; build buying-group engagement; test messages and offers. |
| Days 46–75 │ Convert | Focus on meetings and offer sessions for engaged accounts; reallocate to what is working. |
| Days 76–90 │ Accelerate | Prioritize open opportunities and Tier 1 follow-up; prepare the scale / revise / stop recommendation. |


#### Sales Alert Triggers (Proposed; thresholds Pending Validation)

| TRIGGER | RECOMMENDED SALES ACTION |
| --- | --- |
| Buying-stage movement or surging intent on core topics | BDR/AE reviews the account within the agreed SLA and starts or accelerates outreach. |
| Two or more buying-group members engaged | AE builds the buyer map, assigns persona outreach and proposes a meeting. |
| Pricing, demo or high-intent page activity | Same-day BDR follow-up; offer the matching session. |
| Open opportunity with new engagement | AE re-engages the champion and sponsor; consider Reachdesk acceleration. |

Guardrail: Never imply knowledge of a company’s private security incidents, audit status, budget or hiring plans without reliable public or permissioned evidence.

| --- | --- | --- |

Investment note: No approved acquisition economics, ACV or CAC data exist. Do not publish CAC targets, payback assumptions or pipeline forecasts until Finance and RevOps validate them.


### Measurement & Governance


#### Pilot Scorecard

| MEASURE | DEFINITION / REVIEW |
| --- | --- |
| Pipeline & opportunity creation | Sales-accepted opportunities and associated pipeline; report only verified Salesforce values. Pipeline amount targets pending baseline data. |
| Qualified meetings | Meetings accepted as qualified by Sales; illustrative range of 10–18 in 90 days, to be confirmed. |
| Opportunity progression and velocity | Stage conversion and time between stages for campaign-touched opportunities; track through the 90-day tail. |
| Buying-group penetration | Number and seniority of relevant engaged personas per Tier 1 / Tier 2 account. |
| Account engagement & stage movement | Share of named accounts with meaningful engagement and 6sense buying-stage movement; supporting indicators only. |
| Efficiency | Cost per qualified meeting and channel conversion, assessed once the sample size and approved budget allow. |

Attribution: Define sourced vs. influenced pipeline before launch and apply the rules consistently across the 90-day activation and 90-day tracking tail. Report CRM stage progression alongside channel activity and avoid implying causation from engagement alone.


#### Decision Cadence

Weekly: Marketing, BDR/SDR and AE account review. Day 30: assess engagement, meeting pace and offer uptake. Day 60: refine account prioritization and channel allocation. Day 90: scale / revise / stop recommendation based on verified outcomes. Day 180: pipeline progression and early win-rate and velocity review.
