# Sanitized Example: Cloud Security 1:Few Program

> Fictional example. Organizations, signals, metrics, and activities are illustrative.

## Executive Summary

A 1:few program targets 12 large financial-services organizations modernizing cloud infrastructure while managing regulatory and operational risk. The program uses shared industry pressures with account-level personalization based on verified public information and approved first-party engagement.

## Prioritization

| Dimension | Weight | Illustrative Evidence | Score |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| ICP fit | 30% | Enterprise scale, regulated environment, multi-cloud complexity | 5 |
| Intent | 20% | Research activity across cloud posture and threat detection topics | 4 |
| Engagement | 20% | Security webinar attendance and technical content consumption | 3 |
| Relationships | 15% | Known contacts in security operations; limited executive coverage | 3 |
| Timing | 15% | Public modernization initiative; buying project unconfirmed | 3 |

## Segment Thesis

Security leaders need to support cloud expansion without multiplying disconnected controls or slowing engineering teams. The campaign positions consolidated visibility and faster investigation as business enablers while clearly labeling account-specific priorities as hypotheses until validated.

## Buying-Group Messages

| Role | Primary Value | Proof Needed | Next Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CISO | Reduce exposure while enabling transformation | Executive outcomes and risk evidence | Peer roundtable |
| Security Operations | Improve visibility and investigation speed | Technical workflow and integration proof | Technical workshop |
| Cloud Platform Leader | Protect cloud delivery without added friction | Architecture and deployment guidance | Joint discovery |
| Finance/Procurement | Simplify cost and vendor complexity | Business case and adoption plan | Value review |

## Coordinated Play

1. Marketing launches an industry point-of-view and executive roundtable.
2. BDRs use approved intent and engagement context to prioritize outreach.
3. AEs receive account briefs with buying-group gaps and tailored discovery questions.
4. Engaged accounts receive a technical workshop or value-assessment offer.
5. Marketing and Sales review account progression weekly and change plays based on verified feedback.

## Success Measures

- Buying-group coverage and engaged-account rate
- Meaningful meetings and workshop acceptance
- Account stage progression
- Opportunities created or accelerated
- Pipeline and revenue impact
